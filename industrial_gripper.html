Design and FEA of a 2-Finger Industrial Gripper

Parametric design and static structural validation of a gear-driven parallel jaw mechanism for 10 kg payloads.

1. Problem Statement & Scope
Designed a 2-finger parallel jaw industrial gripper for robotic applications capable of holding payloads between 5 kg and 10 kg. The design required fully parametric 3D modeling of the fingers, base, and actuation interface, followed by rigorous static stress validation under peak gripping loads with a target Factor of Safety (FoS) > 1.5.
2. Mechanism & Kinematics
Implemented a gear-driven dual parallelogram linkage. A rotary actuator drives a primary gear meshed 1:1 with a secondary gear, resulting in symmetric contra-rotation. Integrated crank arms connect to the gripping elements via revolute pin joints. Aligner links prevent finger rotation, enforcing pure parallel Cartesian motion. Hand calculations demanded a normal clamping force of 122.6 N per finger to hold a 10 kg payload securely.
3. Finite Element Analysis (FEA)
Static structural analysis was conducted in ANSYS Workbench 2026 R1. With an adaptive mesh of 78,446 nodes, ramped actuation (61.3 N) and gripping (150 N) loads were applied. The analysis returned a peak von-Mises stress of 73.25 MPa at the linkage arm roots and a maximum tip deflection of 0.38 mm, yielding a minimum FoS of 3.41 against the 250 MPa yield strength of structural steel.
